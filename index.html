NICTSeD 2026 · CyberSAFE Challenge Trophy · Grand Final

AI NATION 2030

Strategically transforming Malaysia into an AI-driven future while upholding ethical governance and human dignity

Our answer has one word: MAHIR. Five rules. One for every part of the plan that protects people.

MAHIR
SMK St Bernadette's Convent, Perak
THE OPENING NUMBER

Second in the world for deepfake losses

Malaysia is building towards the global top ten in AI by 2030. At the same time, Malaysians are already among the most heavily targeted people in the world by AI-enabled fraud. That is why the governance half of AI Nation 2030 matters now, and not only in 2030.

THE QUESTION

The whole question hides in one word

The title makes three demands. The word that decides the winner is not and. It is WHILE. Both must happen at the same time, in the same country, under the same budget, inside the same sixty months.

THE PLAN IS REAL

AI Nation 2030 is a live government document

Launched by the Prime Minister on 28 July 2026 with AI Malaysia Berhad. We are not here to propose a vision. The vision exists. We are here to propose how its governance half can be given the same force as its growth half.

THE GROWTH SIDE

What the plan commits to

Every growth promise is funded, dated and measured.

THE GOVERNANCE HALF

The half that is still being written

Malaysia already has its governance principles. They live in the National Guidelines on AI Governance and Ethics (AIGE), issued by MOSTI in September 2024. Seven strong principles, adopted voluntarily. The government is now converting them into law through the AI Governance Bill. MAHIR is our proposal for what that law should require and measure.

The growth half already has owners, dates and metrics. MAHIR proposes the same three things for the governance half.

THESIS

AI Nation 2030 already tells Malaysia where to go. MAHIR is how we make sure nobody is left behind on the way.

The growth half of the plan is funded, dated and measured. The governance half is being written right now, and that is the moment to get it right. Our proposal does one thing: it gives the governance half the same owners, dates and metrics that the growth half was given.

THE SCALE OF THE TASK

A real start against a very large number

RM110 million for 22,000 training places is a genuine national effort, and it is running today. The exposure it works against is 685,000 workers. MAHIR proposes tying the funding to the number, so the programme can scale with the problem.

THE DIVIDE HAS MOVED

Access is nearly solved. Capability and protection are next.

Almost every Malaysian is online, most of them through a phone. That phone is where deepfake investment scams are delivered. Connectivity without literacy is not inclusion. It is exposure.

THE PRECEDENTS

Malaysia has already legislated harder things

When a judge says our proposals are unrealistic, this is the answer. Five binding Acts in under two years.

THE MOST USEFUL PRECEDENT

A delivery rider can already contest an algorithm

Since 31 March 2026, the Gig Workers Act gives 1.64 million workers the right not to be deactivated by an algorithm without recourse, and a tribunal to hear the complaint. Parliament accepted the principle. We ask for it to be extended to every Malaysian facing a high-stakes automated decision: in health, in credit, in welfare, in school placement.

Why does a loan applicant deserve less protection than a food delivery rider?
THE FRAMEWORK

MAHIR: five rules, five words

The national plan already has a pillar called Trust via Responsible Governance. It lists four enablers, E11 to E14. Good ideas, but nobody remembers a code. Mahir means skilled in Malay. Here it is five rules you can actually hold on to.

M
Manage AI Responsibly
Check how risky an AI is before you switch it on.
A film recommender and a system that decides who gets a loan should not follow the same rules.
A
Accountability
A named human signs off. Never “the system decided”.
When an AI gets it wrong, you know exactly who answers for it.
H

AI suggests. People decide. You can ask a human to look again.
The final call on anything that affects your life stays with a person.
I
Integrity
You can check where AI content came from, and see what the system did.

R
Respecting Human Dignity
Nobody gets left behind, and nobody is treated as just a number.
Rural, elderly, disabled, low income. The benefit has to reach them too.

Five words. One for every part of the plan that protects people.

M · MANAGE AI RESPONSIBLY
MManage AI Responsibly

Check the risk before you switch it on

A spam filter and a system that decides who gets welfare should not follow the same rules. The bigger the risk to a person, the stronger the checks. The plan already names this step: enabler E13, National AI Classification, led by the Ministry of Digital with AI Malaysia. We are saying it should apply before deployment, not after.

M · MANAGE AI RESPONSIBLY
MManage AI Responsibly

Assess the impact, then keep a human in the loop

THE ASSESSMENT

Before a high-risk system is deployed, the organisation assesses seven things: bias, privacy, cybersecurity, accuracy, explainability, the populations affected and potential social harm. AIGE already calls for evaluation across the AI lifecycle, so this operationalises existing guidance rather than duplicating it.

THE HUMAN GATE

For consequential decisions: AI recommends. Humans decide. Not every system needs a person approving every output. Decisions that affect rights or livelihoods do. The assurance body already exists: the Malaysia AI Safety Institute (MY-AISafe) was launched inside AI Malaysia Berhad on 28 July 2026 with a mandate for safety evaluation and red-teaming. MAHIR gives it a gate to stand at.

R · RESPECTING HUMAN DIGNITY
RRespecting Human Dignity

Respect the person. Include every Malaysian.

RESPECT HUMAN RIGHTS

Anyone affected by an AI decision keeps three things: privacy, human agency, and the ability to challenge a consequential decision. Malaysia has already accepted this principle in law. The Gig Workers Act 2025 protects 1.64 million workers against discriminatory algorithmic deactivation and gives them a tribunal.

UPLIFT THROUGH INCLUSION

The benefit cannot stop at the Klang Valley, large corporations and the digitally confident. MAHIR measures participation by rural communities, elderly citizens, persons with disabilities, low-income households, SMEs and people with limited digital literacy. This follows the plan’s own direction: AI Nation 2030 is explicit that the benefits must be inclusive, not adoption for its own sake.

R · RESPECTING HUMAN DIGNITY
RRespecting Human Dignity

Reskill before retrenchment, not after

THE USUAL SEQUENCE

AI is introduced. Employees are displaced. Retraining is offered afterwards, if at all. By then the person has already lost the income, the household has already absorbed the shock, and the country has already lost skills it paid to build. Most automation programmes follow this order because no framework has yet asked employers to plan the transition first.

THE MAHIR SEQUENCE

Announce the implementation, identify the vulnerable tasks, run a skills-gap assessment, reskill pre-emptively, redesign the job, deploy human and AI together, and use transition assistance only where displacement truly cannot be avoided. Fund it by tying the reskilling budget to a published displacement estimate per sector, routed through the HRD Corp levy employers already pay. Automate the task. Keep the person.

A · I · ACCOUNTABILITY AND INTEGRITY
AAccountability and Integrity

Somebody’s name is on it, and everything is on the record

NAMED ACCOUNTABILITY

Every high-risk AI system has a clearly identified responsible organisation and an accountable officer. Never “the algorithm decided.” There is always an accountable human institution behind a consequential outcome.

TRACEABILITY AND PROVENANCE

Keep the record: the system, the data used, the recommendation made, the human approval and every change since. Then monitor continuously for incidents, bias and model drift. The same principle applies to what AI produces in public: machine-readable provenance on AI-generated media and cryptographic verification of official government channels, so a citizen can check whether a minister actually said it. That is the preventive layer against the pattern that has already cost Malaysians USD 502 million.

H · HUMAN IN COMMAND
HHuman in Command

One page the country can check every quarter

If an AI decision affects you, you should be able to understand why, and ask a real person to look again. Then the country publishes whether that is actually happening. The plan already tracks growth: Top 10 in global AI indices, +1.2 percentage points of GDP, up to 300,000 new jobs by 2030. We are asking for one more page next to it, so trust gets counted too.

We are not asking the country to start over. Enablers E11 to E14 are already written. We are asking for one thing: adopt MAHIR as the plain-language version of that pillar, so every Malaysian, including a sixteen year old, can understand it and hold it to account.

CLOSE

Manage the technology. Respect the people. Keep every decision answerable to a human.

MAHIR does not slow Malaysia’s AI transformation. It makes that transformation trustworthy. MA ensures we govern AI before it can cause harm. RU ensures people are protected and empowered while technology changes their working lives. AH ensures that when AI has consequences, accountability never disappears behind an algorithm. Every quarter, AI Malaysia Berhad will report what AI added to our economy. We are asking for one more page in that same report, so every Malaysian can see the same progress the economy sees.

AI Nation 2030 tells us what Malaysia wants to become. MAHIR is how we would know we stayed ourselves while becoming it.
